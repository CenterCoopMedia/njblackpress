Historical highlights

Pioneering voices that defined their eras and shaped public discourse.

1880s 01

The Sentinel

Trenton, NJ • 1880–1883

Trenton's first Black newspaper, founded by R. Henri Herbert in 1880. A pioneering voice in the post-Reconstruction era. Herbert ran it about three years, then sold it to Colonel E. C. Stahl, who consolidated it with the Trenton Herald; no read source gives a year for the consolidation.

1880s 02

New Jersey Trumpet

Jersey City / Newark • 1887–1897

Founded by William Murrell, this weekly advocated for civil rights, economic advancement, and political participation during rapid urban growth and discrimination.

1900s 03

The Echo

Long Branch / Red Bank • 1904–1943

Described as "the oldest colored paper in New Jersey" — a house boast the archive has not verified, and one the New Jersey Herald News also made. Served the Jersey Shore's Black communities for nearly four decades during the Jim Crow era.

1930s 04

New Jersey Guardian

Newark, NJ • 1934–1942

"Voice of the Colored People." Represented Newark's Black community during the Great Depression and early World War II, edited by F. R. Clark.

1940s 05

NJ Afro-American

Newark, NJ • 1941–1991

The Newark edition of the Baltimore-based AFRO chain and a major voice during the Civil Rights era. The New Jersey edition no longer exists.

1960s 06

Black Newark

Newark, NJ • 1968–1974

"The Voice of New Ark's Inner City." Emerged during intense political organizing following the 1967 Newark uprising, published by the Committee for Unified Newark.

Contemporary voices

Today's Black-owned media outlets continuing the legacy of community journalism.

2021–present

Trenton Journal

Trenton, NJ

Founded by Kenneth Miles after community listening sessions. Solutions-based journalism for Trenton's majority Black and Latino community.

Visit trentonjournal.com →
2017–present

Ark Republic

Newark, NJ

Founded by Dr. Kaia Niambi Shivers. Multimedia journalism amplifying underrepresented voices through podcasts, photo essays, and creative storytelling.

Visit arkrepublic.com →
2018–present

New Jersey Urban News

Newark, NJ

"A Voice for the Voiceless." Co-founded by Penda Howell and Dorothy Howell. Your Black news source in the Garden State, covering stories overlooked by mainstream media.

Visit njurbannews.com →
2021–present

Black In Jersey

Trenton, NJ

"Our Voices, Our Perspectives." Founded by Tennyson Coleman. A statewide digital publication focused on Black community news and culture.

Trenton, NJ

Founded by Kamau Kujichagulia. A bilingual Trenton paper that describes itself as "New Jersey's only African-American-Hispanic news source." Its January 7, 2022 issue is numbered Volume 6, No. 1, so the length of its run is unsettled.

About the project

The New Jersey Black Press Database is a digital preservation initiative by the Center for Cooperative Media, dedicated to documenting the history and ongoing contributions of Black-owned news publications throughout the Garden State.

From post-Reconstruction weeklies of the 1880s to contemporary digital outlets, the Black Press has documented community life, challenged discrimination, and amplified voices often overlooked by mainstream media. New Jersey’s proximity to New York and Philadelphia shaped a distinct press tradition rooted in local advocacy, civic participation, and cultural preservation.

This project highlights major publications, influential editors, and the themes that defined coverage — civil rights reporting, local news, political engagement, and cultural arts — while also reflecting on the policy landscape that has shaped Black-owned media, from historic barriers to today’s digital and regulatory challenges.

By preserving these archives and stories, the database underscores the Black Press’s enduring role in informing and empowering New Jersey’s Black communities, and it supports ongoing efforts to sustain this vital media legacy.

Curated by the Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University.
