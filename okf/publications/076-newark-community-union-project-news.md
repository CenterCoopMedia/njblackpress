## Historical notes

The Newark Community Union Project produced a newsletter, but its exact title and first and final publication years remain unverified. Danky and Hady, entry 4335, page 417, gives the uncertain range 1963?–1965? and a Wisconsin Historical Society reference, Pam 1719. Those question-mark dates must not appear as exact years. WorldCat OCLC 261342029 catalogs an NCUP newsletter without an exact publication year. New Left Notes of March 11, 1966 describes the organizers’ arrival in summer 1964; that is a project date, not the newsletter’s founding date. Its August 5, 1966 issue attributes a reprinted Peter Countryman article to the NCUP newsletter. The reprint establishes the newsletter’s existence before that date, not publication during 1966 or its final year. Cornell’s Walter Thabit papers, collection 4215, box 4, folder 23, provides another newsletter lead. NCUP organized in Newark’s Clinton Hill neighborhood as part of SDS’s Economic Research and Action Project. The project focused on housing issues, organizing rent strikes (which were illegal in New Jersey), picketing slumlords' homes, and conducting block meetings to address rats, roaches, unsafe living conditions, and police misconduct. NCUP's work is documented in the 1966 documentary film 'Troublemakers.' By late summer 1965, the project staff had dwindled to about half a dozen organizers, though an estimated 150 local people participated in various programs and meetings. The 1967 Newark Rebellion effectively ended NCUP, as the uprising convinced the remaining white organizers they were unwanted. NCUP's methods -- rent strikes, picketing, block meetings -- had by then spread to other parts of the city. Archival materials may exist at the NYU Tamiment Library, Rutgers University Libraries, or the Library of Congress, which catalogs materials under the subject heading 'Newark Community Union Project (N.J.)'.
