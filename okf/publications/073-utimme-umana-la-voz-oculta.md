## Historical notes

This record represents the combined title Utimme Umana/La Voz Oculta at Trenton State College, now The College of New Jersey. TCNJ dates the earlier Black student paper Utimme Umana to 1968 and the combined title’s first issue to October 2, 1972. The 1972 field follows the title named by this record; 1968 remains its predecessor date. TCNJ describes a change from newspaper to magazine format in 1978. A surviving volume 25, number 3 issue bears February 1993 on its cover, contradicting the former 1990 end year. A September 27, 1993 Trenton Times account also names the minority magazine. No final publication year is established. The publication served Black and Hispanic students through poetry, short stories, essays, campus reporting, photographs, and artwork. Schedules varied across its history; the earlier six-times-a-year field is not used for the whole run. Digitized issues are available through TCNJ. Its founding was part of a broader movement at Trenton State College where, following the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr. in April 1968, African American students presented a list of 10 demands to President Robert Heussler, including creation of an Afro-American Studies program, increased Black student enrollment, hiring Black entertainment, and having a dedicated space on campus for Black students.
